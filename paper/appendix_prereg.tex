\section{The pre-registered thresholds, verbatim}
\label{app:prereg}

This paper uses the words ``pre-registered'', ``fixed in advance'' and
``registered before'' about seventy times and, until this appendix, reproduced
no registered threshold anywhere. The thirty-eight documents in \path{prereg/}
are the record those phrases refer to. Each was committed to the repository
before the material it governs was played: before the arm existed, before the
runner existed, or --- where a screening measurement had already been taken ---
with that measurement stated inside the document so it could not be
re-described afterwards. The table below reproduces, for every one of them, the
decision rule as written, whether the document stated a power limit in advance,
what the run returned, and where in this paper the verdict appears.

The registrations that returned nulls, refutations, withdrawals and
non-results are listed on exactly the same footing as the ones that returned
effects, in the same table, in filename order. Eight of the thirty-eight
have \emph{no outcome recorded in the registration document itself}; six of
those eight were nonetheless run and are reported in this paper or in
\path{RESEARCH_FRONTIER.md}, and two have not been run at all. Both facts are
marked in the table rather than smoothed over, because a registration that is
written and never resolved is the file-drawer risk this appendix exists to
expose. Anything that could not be verified against a committed results file
is marked \textbf{unverified}; it is not presented as though it had been.

Throughout: ``pairs'' are duplicate-deal paired games with the pair as the
independent unit; ``ship bar'' is this project's standing $+0.15$ sets/game
threshold; a quoted string is verbatim from the registration.

\noindent\textbf{How the thirty-eight resolved.} Seven returned an effect that
still stands on the rule as registered (P2, P5, P11, P14, P20, P26, P33). Five
returned an effect and then lost it to later evidence, in every case by a rule

\textbf{refuted} on a registered condition (P1, P4, P7, P10, P23, P28, P32,
P36). Five returned an explicit null, an explicit \textsc{split}, or an

or by proof before the registered duel was paid for, saving roughly $9{,}000$
duplicate-deal pairs (P3, P12, P29, P34). One was a set of graded predictions
rather than a threshold and scored four of five (P25). And eight have

ship bar was the wrong instrument for it.

\subsection{The six conditions the headline claims rest on}

The rules below are quoted in full because they are load-bearing: each one is
the reason a claim elsewhere in this paper is stated the way it is, and in
every case the rule was written before the number that would be judged against
it existed.

\paragraph{P20, \protect\path{prereg/forced_exhaustive.md} --- the only change in this
round that shipped, and the only one that refused the standing bar in advance.}

\begin{quote}
``\textbf{THE DECISION RULE IS NOT THIS PROJECT'S USUAL ONE, and here is why,
in advance.} The predicted margin effect is $+0.028$ sets/game \ldots Against a
paired-difference SD near $1.15$ that needs roughly $12{,}000$ games to resolve
from zero. Every duel this project could afford would return a null, and that
null would read as `no effect' when the truth is `an effect two orders of
magnitude below what this instrument can see'. So the ship bar of $+0.15$
sets/game \ldots is \textbf{the wrong instrument here and is deliberately not
used.} \ldots \textbf{Primary outcome, fixed now.} Forced-declaration accuracy
at one live half-suit, arm B against arm A on identical deals, fresh seeds,
both parities. Ships if accuracy rises with the interval clear of zero.
\textbf{Secondary outcome, fixed now.} The paired margin against v0.7 with its
interval, reported whatever it says, and \textbf{explicitly not a ship
criterion}.''
\end{quote}

\noindent The accuracy claim of \S\ref{sec:res-pathledger} is exactly this
primary. The registration also honoured its own secondary the hard way: the
first self-play run gave every seat the arm, producing ``$+0.0000$, 95\%
$[-0.0142, +0.0142]$ \ldots an arithmetic identity wearing a confidence
interval'', and nothing shipped until the registered v0.7 arm was actually run.

\paragraph{P23, \protect\path{prereg/gamma_split.md} --- the two-condition belief rule
that every later belief measurement in this paper inherited.}

\begin{quote}
``A cell \textbf{licenses a play experiment} only if, on the teammate pool:
1.\ its paired NLL interval lies \textbf{entirely below zero} (a real
improvement in the proper score), \textbf{and} 2.\ its paired top-1 interval is
\textbf{not entirely below zero} (it does not significantly worsen how often
the true holder is named). \textbf{Both conditions, or nothing.} \ldots A model
can improve NLL purely by spreading probability mass while naming the holder
correctly less often. For a policy whose dominant error is \emph{naming a
split}, that is a worse belief, not a better one --- the allocation decision
reads the argmax, not the entropy. \textbf{If no cell satisfies both, the
direction is withdrawn and no play experiment is run.}''
\end{quote}

and it was then imposed unchanged on the convention (P5), on the aimed and
locating code books (P6, P9), and on \texttt{unlocated\_now} in the belief

all this rule firing.
